AI Creators Hackathon

Why you're here today
There are two kinds of people in this room, and both are equally welcome.
Some of you are here because you want a seat at FlytBase. We're actually hiring —
and we've found that jamming with someone on a real problem tells us far more than an
interview ever could. If that's you, working on the NestGen track and doing it well is
genuinely one of the best ways to show us what you've got.
Some of you are here to learn, build your network, and get inspired. That's just as
valid — work on whatever idea excites you.
Either way, everyone's output gets judged the same way — on taste, storytelling, and
the AI hacks you actually pulled off. Winning or losing isn't really the point. What matters
is being honest with yourself about why you're here today, and picking the track that fits
that.
What this hackathon is
You've just watched a morning of state-of-the-art AI video creation, and seen the team's own
hacks — including the Katputli Factory. Now it's your turn to build something real by 5:00 PM.
You have two options. Read both before you pick.
Option 1 — NestGen (recommended)
Build something that markets NestGen '26, FlytBase's global event. This is the
recommended path — it's the one being judged most closely, and it comes with a full

pack of context, real stories, real speaker names, and images so you're not starting from
zero.
If you pick this option: go to the shared Drive folder (link below) and download 
NestGen26_Context.md. That file has everything — what NestGen is, why it matters,
real industry stories, confirmed speakers, and a full image bank. Open it in your AI tool of
choice and keep working from there — treat it as the living brief you jam on for the rest of
the build.
Option 2 — Your own idea
If you've already got an idea for a video, a site, or a skill you want to bring to life today, go
with that instead. It doesn't need to be about NestGen at all.
Got an idea but need inspiration? Ask the team or mentors in the room for references of
the latest AI-generated videos — use them as a style base, not a script to copy.
What we're judging (applies to both options)
AI models are good enough now that almost anyone can generate something that looks
aesthetic. That's not the bar anymore. The real question is what you did with it:
Storytelling — does it actually say something, or is it just a pretty generation with
nothing behind it?
Taste & attention to detail — the small choices: pacing, timing, what you included and
cut, whether it feels considered.
Engagement — would someone actually watch it to the end, or want to keep scrolling
your site?
The hack — how clever or unique was your technique or workflow, and how fast did you
get to a working result?
Fit — for NestGen: does it make someone want to know more? For your own idea: does
it land clearly?
The model will make it look good almost by default. The question we're actually asking is:
did you make it good — did you tell a story, did you make a choice, did you make us feel
something?
